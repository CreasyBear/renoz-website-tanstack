# WA off-grid buyer demand research

- Market: residential and rural off-grid power systems
- Buyer: WA property owner or operator researching a professionally designed system
- Geography: Australia, with Western Australia and Perth modifiers checked separately
- Research date: 2026-09-01
- Claim constraints: no savings, payback, rebate, price, reliability, superiority, or performance promise in advertising

## Method and evidence

This is a qualitative intent study, not a keyword-volume report. It combines Australian Google Autocomplete observations, current Australian search-result page types, and public Australian buyer questions. It also uses the GooseWorks pattern of mining angles from buyer evidence before creative production.

Autocomplete was queried with `hl=en` and `gl=au`. Representative current results were inspected for cost, sizing, battery comparison, and WA installer searches. Public community questions were used for language and decision context, not as technical authority.

## What people are trying to decide

1. **What will a complete system cost?** Cost, price, package, and quote modifiers appear throughout battery and complete-system suggestions. Search results answer with guides, calculators, and commercial comparison pages.
2. **What size system does this property need?** Searchers ask for a battery size, house size, system calculator, and daily-use calculation. Their questions commonly include daily kWh, peak loads, winter production, cloudy days, and autonomy.
3. **How do the components work together?** Searchers look for solar packages with batteries and generator backup, not only a standalone battery. Community questions repeatedly connect battery capacity with panels, inverter capability, peak loads, and generator policy.
4. **Which battery or system is suitable?** “Best”, comparison, type, lithium, and reviews language shows a solution-aware research stage. This cluster needs precise evidence and education, not a generic superiority claim.
5. **Who can design or install it locally?** Installer, near-me, Perth, Western Australia, and WA variants appear, although some WA seeds have heavy “water” and “wall” noise. Locality is a conversion and trust modifier more than the strongest opening hook.
6. **Does a generator still belong in the plan?** Generator-backup suggestions and buyer discussions are frequent enough to support a distinct architecture angle.

## Implications for advertising

The campaign should intercept the decisions above rather than lead with “farm battery” or “Wheatbelt battery”. Those phrases are relevant to RENOZ targeting and imagery, but they showed weak direct suggestion depth.

The first Meta batch should therefore test three different buyer decisions:

- sizing: “Start with the loads”;
- architecture: “Plan solar, battery and generator as one system”; and
- proof: a factual Harvey, WA system record for buyers comparing options.

These are solution-aware and high-consideration ads. Their job is to earn a qualified visit or project discussion, not generate broad awareness or promise an outcome.

## Exclusions and noise

Caravan systems, portable power stations, DIY kits, solar water pumps, international results, on-grid-only batteries, and “water” or “wall” collisions were excluded. “Farm” and named WA region phrases remain useful audience context but were not treated as demonstrated high-volume search hooks.

## Limitations

Autocomplete and public search results do not provide monthly volume, competition, cost per click, or conversion value. Google Ads Keyword Planner and RENOZ Search Console, enquiry, and CRM exports are required for quantitative prioritisation. Community posts reveal concerns but do not prove that the advice in replies is technically correct.

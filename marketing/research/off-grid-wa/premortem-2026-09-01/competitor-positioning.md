# Competitor positioning: regional WA off-grid power

**Research date:** 1 September 2026  
**Scope:** Residential, rural-property, agricultural and smaller commercial buyers in Western Australia who may need a complete off-grid, fringe-of-grid or generator-hybrid power solution.

This is a positioning audit, not a product-performance assessment. Competitor performance, savings and reliability statements below are treated as their claims unless independently evidenced. Meta's public Ad Library and Google's Ads Transparency Center did not expose a dependable advertiser-level result through the available research interface, so this report does not infer active media, spend or winning creative.

## Executive read

The buyer is not choosing among battery brands alone. They are choosing among four different forms of accountability:

1. **A turnkey WA integrator** that will assess loads, design, supply, install and support the complete system.
2. **A battery or BESS manufacturer** whose installer or distributor network delivers the job.
3. **A component-led architecture** built around an inverter, battery and generator combination selected by an experienced installer.
4. **A substitute** such as a network-provided stand-alone power system, grid extension, retaining diesel, or postponing the project.

For the target buyer, Statewide Power and OffGrid WA are the clearest direct competitors because they sell an installed WA outcome. PowerPlus Energy, RedEarth, genZ and Renox are strong product or BESS brands, but their degree of end-to-end project ownership varies. Selectronic is an influential component and trust signal rather than a complete retail alternative. Western Power's stand-alone power program can remove the need for a privately purchased system for eligible properties.

## Required competitors

### PowerPlus Energy

**Role and buyer.** PowerPlus is an Australian battery/BESS manufacturer, not normally the party that performs a homeowner's installation. Its site explicitly sends homeowners and businesses to certified installers, while installers and project teams can source through authorised distributors. It spans residential, commercial, off-grid and remote projects. [Where to buy](https://www.powerplus-energy.com.au/where-to-buy/) · [Battery range](https://www.powerplus-energy.com.au/products/batteries/)

**Promise.** Its strongest frame is dependable Australian energy storage built for real conditions: locally manufactured batteries, compliant cabinets/BESS, local technical support, scalability and installer compatibility. Its residential messaging has moved closer to customer pain—blackouts, storing solar for night use and maintaining normal life—while the broader brand remains engineering- and product-led. [Residential positioning](https://www.powerplus-energy.com.au/residential/) · [Company history](https://www.powerplus-energy.com.au/about/)

**Proof and trust.** PowerPlus uses Australian manufacturing, certification, technical downloads, installer training, warranty registration and a deep case-study library. Its WA proof includes a 96 kWh off-grid glamping project near Busselton, a Pilbara research site and a Lake Deborah battery replacement. The case studies often name the installer and complete architecture, making PowerPlus look like the storage backbone of a designed system rather than a commodity module. [Willow Wood WA case study](https://www.powerplus-energy.com.au/case-study/off-grid-glamping-power-solution-willow-wood-retreat/) · [Pilbara case study](https://www.powerplus-energy.com.au/case-study/reliable-off-grid-energy-storage-for-a-remote-renewables-research-site-in-the-pilbara/) · [Lake Deborah case study](https://www.powerplus-energy.com.au/case-study/lake-deborah-wild-harvested-salt-and-renewables/)

**CTA and distribution.** “Find an installer,” “where to buy” and “speak to a team member.” This reduces direct project ownership but gives PowerPlus national reach and lets trusted local installers carry the conversion.

**Likely strength.** The combination of Australian manufacture, installer familiarity, technical proof and visible WA deployments is a strong risk-reduction story for remote buyers.

**Likely gap.** A property owner still needs confidence in the local designer/installer and in the complete system scope. Manufacturer credibility does not by itself answer who owns travel, switchboard work, generator integration, commissioning and after-hours faults.

### genZ Energy

**Role and buyer.** genZ is a Perth-headquartered Australian battery innovator and supplier. It addresses mining, transport, agriculture, government, defence, residential, commercial, off-grid and hybrid applications. Its warranty refers to products sold through genZ or authorised distribution partners/dealers, but the public site foregrounds a direct “get a quote” form rather than a consumer installer locator. [About genZ](https://www.genz.com.au/about-us/) · [Contact](https://www.genz.com.au/contact-us/) · [Warranty](https://www.genz.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/genZ-Battery-Warranty-Rackmount-Jan2023-rev2.2.pdf)

**Promise.** “Energy storage you rely on”: cost-effective, safe LFP batteries engineered for harsh Australian conditions. The distinctive product story is cylindrical-cell LFP, built-in/self-managed BMS, lead-acid replacement, light weight, safety and long life. Agriculture is framed around irrigation, machinery, remote operations and backup controls. [Home page](https://www.genz.com.au/) · [Mining, transport and agriculture](https://www.genz.com.au/industrial/) · [Product range](https://www.genz.com.au/products/)

**Proof and trust.** Perth headquarters, national offices, claimed use across government/defence/mining/emergency services, CEC listing, ISO accreditations, technical documents and warranties. These are institutional and engineering trust devices rather than homeowner narratives.

**CTA and distribution.** Direct specialist quote, with downstream supply through authorised partners/dealers.

**Likely strength.** “Perth + mission critical + harsh outback” is credible shorthand for a regional buyer worried about heat, isolation and component failure. It can also speak to agricultural loads without changing brand identity.

**Likely gap.** The public residential journey is product-heavy and generic. It gives little visible guidance on installed solution cost, site assessment, generator architecture, regional travel/support or who accepts whole-of-system responsibility. For a buyer asking “who will make this work at my property?”, the route is less explicit than the WA integrators' route.

### Renox Technology — exact entity resolved

The relevant entity is **Renox Technology**, formerly **Redx Technology**. Renox announced the Redx-to-Renox rebrand in August 2025. This is distinct from **RENOZ Energy**. Renox describes itself as an Australian-owned renewable-energy technology parent company; its current contact page lists a Queensland head office and a Canning Vale, WA office. [Rebrand announcement](https://renox.tech/blog/redx-is-now-renox/) · [About](https://renox.tech/about/) · [Contact](https://renox.tech/contact/)

**Role and buyer.** Renox develops inverters, batteries, integrated BESS and AI energy-management software. It addresses homes and businesses, primarily through bill optimisation, backup, solar retrofit and energy trading, while several products claim off-grid capability. Its own package document says “Designed in Australia / Manufactured in China.” [Residential product range](https://renox.tech/residential-products/) · [Renox AI package document](https://renox.tech/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Renox-AI-Energy-Plan-20250826-v1.01.pdf)

**Promise.** “Australia's smart energy company”: proprietary power electronics plus AI that monitors, forecasts, optimises and trades energy. Its aggressive consumer landing page leads with “Say goodbye to your energy bill,” blackout backup, fanless operation, plug-and-play installation and local support. [RX-7000Plus landing page](https://renox.tech/rx-7000plus-landing/) · [RX-7000Plus product](https://renox.tech/bess/rx-7000plus/)

**Proof and trust.** Founder and patent story, Australian engineering, Griffith University research partnership, CATL-branded storage in the RX-7000Plus, warranty options, technical documentation, software/app control and local offices. It also publishes a tiny-home off-grid case narrative. [About Renox](https://renox.tech/about/) · [Tiny-home case](https://renox.tech/an-off-grid-solution-for-a-tiny-home/)

**CTA and distribution.** Product enquiry/free quote. Installation and monitoring setup are installer-mediated.

**Likely strength.** A polished integrated-product and software story is attractive to buyers who want a recognisable appliance, monitoring app and grid-price optimisation as well as backup.

**Likely gap.** The dominant message is grid economics and smart-home functionality, not regional WA project discovery. The site gives much less evidence of sizing complex rural loads, coordinating generators and trades, or supporting distant installations than the specialist integrators.

**Important relationship.** PowerPlus says it established a technology partnership with Renox in 2025 and uses Renox engineering in products such as Whispr. Renox is therefore not a cleanly independent rival to PowerPlus; it is also a technology partner inside the same wider ecosystem. [PowerPlus company history and partnerships](https://www.powerplus-energy.com.au/about/)

**Brand risk for RENOZ.** The near-identical names create organic search, referral and word-of-mouth confusion. Renox's broader product catalogue and older Redx footprint may cause prospects to attribute the wrong products, warranties or claims to RENOZ. RENOZ should consistently use “RENOZ Energy — Western Australia” and show its exact legal/contact identity near lead forms and proof pages.

## Direct installed-solution competitors

### Statewide Power

**Role and buyer.** A WA electrical contractor and turnkey integrator that says it designs, integrates, manufactures and installs solar, batteries and diesel gensets across regional WA for homes, agriculture, business, industry and mining. [Home page](https://statewidepower.com.au/)

**Promise.** “Off-grid solar system experts in Perth & regional WA,” with tier-one components, systems built for tough conditions, custom or turnkey delivery and more than 25 years' experience. The sales journey makes the whole system tangible with “budget” and “premium” packages. Current claimed starting points run from $20,000 for a small Sigenergy package to $99,000 for an extra-large Selectronic package. Those figures are competitor price anchors, not validated like-for-like costs. [Packages and positioning](https://statewidepower.com.au/)

**Proof and trust.** This is the strongest visible WA project library in the set: Gingin small farm, Koorda agricultural capacity upgrade, Albany strawberries, Harvey dairy, stations, pumps, Main Roads and remote infrastructure. Pages often show continuous/peak power, solar, battery and generator capacity—not merely a finished cabinet photo. [Projects](https://statewidepower.com.au/projects/) · [Gingin farm](https://statewidepower.com.au/projects/small-farm-gingin/) · [Agricultural systems](https://statewidepower.com.au/agricultural-off-grid-systems/)

**CTA and distribution.** Direct free quote. It owns the customer relationship and can also supply diesel.

**Likely strength.** It answers “have you done a property like mine in WA?” and “can one party own the whole job?” better than most manufacturers.

**Likely gap.** Its breadth from small homes to 10 MW projects and heavy use of savings/ROI claims can make the residential path feel commercial. It also exposes high premium price anchors before fully explaining scope, which may repel or prematurely sort prospects.

### OffGrid WA

**Role and buyer.** A Wangara-based WA designer, supplier and installer for off-grid, hybrid and grid-connected systems. Its package categories explicitly address workshops/live-in sheds, weekenders, typical homes, farms, stations, small businesses and large properties. [Home and quote pathway](https://offgridwa.com.au/) · [Quote/package page](https://offgridwa.com.au/get-a-quote/)

**Promise.** Energy independence through a system matched to the property. Its strongest acquisition mechanism is price and scope transparency: micro packages at a claimed $13,500–$19,900, economy at $18,000–$29,500, premium at $35,000–$80,000 and deluxe at $80,000+, plus a free load calculator. Again, these are competitor-published anchors, not comparable quotes. [Package ranges](https://offgridwa.com.au/get-a-quote/) · [Load-calculator navigation and system offer](https://offgridwa.com.au/solar-power-perth/)

**Proof and trust.** Detailed Google-review feeds and testimonials repeatedly emphasise explanation, right-sizing, communication, workmanship and support. Its quote form asks location, property type, package preference, motivation, switchboard photos and electricity bill—evidence that it qualifies the installation, not just the battery. [Quote form and qualification fields](https://offgridwa.com.au/solar-power-perth/) · [Farm-oriented reviews and packages](https://offgridwa.com.au/system-packages/micro-offgrid-system-packages/)

**CTA and distribution.** Direct quote, with a calculator/education path before sales.

**Likely strength.** It reduces buyer uncertainty early: “what category am I, roughly what might it cost, and what information do you need?” Its testimonials directly counter oversizing and high-pressure sales anxiety.

**Likely gap.** The “energy independence / save money / uninterrupted power” messaging is broad and sometimes overclaims. Package labels may invite false like-for-like comparison when switchboard work, trenching, structures, travel, generator and load changes make the installed solution site-specific.

### Off-Grid Energy Australia

**Role and buyer.** A national specialist designer and installer with teams and consultants around Australia. Its listed offices are in SA, NSW and Victoria; the public contact page does not list a WA workshop, so actual mobilisation and service economics should be checked per postcode. [Contact and service footprint](https://www.offgridenergy.com.au/contact-us/)

**Promise.** Specialist competence and risk avoidance. Its positioning warns that poor component selection or under-designed systems can be disastrous and foregrounds accreditation, standards, safe installation and an experienced off-grid team. [Company profile](https://www.offgridenergy.com.au/about-us/) · [Battery-storage information](https://www.offgridenergy.com.au/info-centre/battery-storage-faq/)

**Proof and trust.** Long-form education, standards detail, accredited designers/installers, awards and a claim to be the largest provider of Selectronic off-grid systems. It also documents utility-connection processes, including Western Power and Horizon Power. [Company profile](https://www.offgridenergy.com.au/about-us/) · [WA connection guide](https://www.offgridenergy.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/How-To-Guide-PTC-Connections.pdf)

**Likely strength.** It makes expertise itself the product, which is persuasive for a buyer afraid of an under-sized or non-compliant system.

**Likely gap.** A regional WA buyer may prefer a clearly local team with visible WA projects and an explicit service radius. That is a verification question, not proof they cannot serve WA.

### Allied Solar

**Role and buyer.** A WA designer/fabricator of packaged off-grid systems with both direct contact and an installer-reseller offer.

**Promise.** A simple, relocatable, outdoor-rated cabinet containing battery, power conversion and safety equipment. Its most emotionally specific feature is battery-state prediction that can start the generator before night rather than waking occupants later. It says systems are engineered, fabricated, assembled and tested in Perth. [Allied Solar off-grid systems](https://alliedsolar.com.au/)

**Proof and trust.** System-level technical explanation, named local contact, local build/support and an invitation to see units operating.

**Likely strength.** Concrete operational detail makes the system easy to imagine living with. The generator-night scenario speaks more directly to off-grid life than generic “energy independence.”

**Likely gap.** The public presentation and two-year warranty claim are weaker trust devices than competitors offering larger project libraries, installer directories, modern certifications and longer product warranties.

## Product-platform and upper-end alternatives

### RedEarth Energy Storage

RedEarth is an Australian BESS manufacturer whose brand spans tiny homes, weekenders, whole homes, farms, cattle stations and large commercial sites. Its core positioning is “Take your power back” and “Built in Australia, for Australia,” supported by local manufacture, harsh-condition suitability, all-in-one systems, local engineering, monitoring and warranties. Products such as BlackMax and BushChook make off-grid compatibility and generator connection legible to households, while Dingo targets farms and larger operations. [Home](https://redearth.energy/) · [Residential systems](https://redearth.energy/residential/) · [Dingo](https://redearth.energy/dingo/)

RedEarth explicitly does not sell direct to the public; it routes buyers to a national authorised-installer network by postcode. This gives reach but moves installation accountability to the local partner. [Installer locator](https://redearth.energy/find-an-installer/)

**Competitive significance:** RedEarth is the clearest example of a manufacturer packaging the *whole BESS* rather than just modules. Its Australian-made story, memorable product architecture and installer network can neutralise RENOZ's “local battery” advantage unless RENOZ makes the WA project/service layer more concrete.

### Sunrise Energy Group

Sunrise is a renewable project developer rather than a mainstream residential solar retailer. It frames SPS as a customised combination of solar, BESS and diesel generator for mines, businesses and homes avoiding or awaiting grid connection. It highlights site requirements, budget, remote monitoring, remote fault diagnosis and ongoing maintenance. [Standalone Power Systems](https://sunriseenergygroup.com.au/services/standalone-power-systems/)

**Competitive significance:** It is more relevant as project complexity and operating consequence rise. For a large farm or regional business, engineering studies, remote operations and maintenance may matter more than battery price.

### Selectronic

Selectronic manufactures the Australian-made SP PRO multi-mode inverter/charger, described as the heart of a battery-based system. Its component approach supports off-grid/grid-connected architectures with solar, wind, hydro, microgrids and diesel generators. [Selectronic home](https://www.selectronic.com.au/) · [Support](https://www.selectronic.com.au/support/)

**Competitive significance:** It is not a battery-company or turnkey-installer competitor, but it is a powerful ingredient brand. Statewide, OffGrid WA, PowerPlus and RedEarth all use or reference Selectronic in premium off-grid contexts. A serious buyer or installer may first decide “Selectronic/Victron architecture” and only then choose the battery. RENOZ therefore competes to be specified inside an architecture, not only to be chosen as a brand.

## Substitutes that change the addressable market

### Western Power stand-alone power systems

Western Power supplies selected regional customers with a utility-owned SPS comprising solar, battery and backup generator. Western Power says it pays for the transition and operates and maintains the unit. This is not a retail option a customer can simply buy, but it can remove eligible properties from the private-system market. The utility also frames SPS around reliability and reduced bushfire exposure, both highly credible regional pain points. [Western Power SPS](https://www.westernpower.com.au/resources-education/our-network-the-grid/grid-technology/stand-alone-power-system/)

### Grid extension or upgrade

Competitors repeatedly position off-grid against an expensive or unavailable network connection. The customer will compare not only capital cost but responsibility, future capacity, reliability and property value. Statewide's Gingin and Koorda case studies and OffGrid WA's Bullsbrook testimonial make this comparison explicit. [Statewide projects](https://statewidepower.com.au/projects/) · [OffGrid WA package/testimonial page](https://offgridwa.com.au/system-packages/micro-offgrid-system-packages/)

### Keep or expand diesel generation

Diesel remains the familiar fallback and may be the financially safer short-term decision when the project is uncertain, seasonal or cash constrained. The best competitors do not pretend the generator disappears: Statewide sells and integrates gensets; Sunrise defines a normal SPS as solar + battery + diesel; Allied automates generator timing; Western Power includes backup generation. This makes “remove diesel” a weaker and less credible frame than “decide what role the generator should play.”

### Delay, reduce loads or stage the build

The cheapest near-term choice may be to defer the system, build only a shed/weekender package, change electric loads, retain LPG/diesel appliances or stage solar and storage. Package ladders from OffGrid WA and modular manufacturer platforms make this option visible. A lead program that demands an all-at-once project will lose buyers who can pay only through staging.

## Positioning patterns and openings

### What is already crowded

- **Australian designed/made and built for harsh conditions.** PowerPlus, genZ, RedEarth, Allied, Selectronic and RENOZ can all tell a local/toughness story.
- **Energy independence, lower bills and blackout protection.** Nearly every residential brand uses these promises; they are category language, not differentiation.
- **Scalable/modular LFP.** Common across the product set.
- **Local support and long warranties.** Necessary trust signals, but only differentiating when the service mechanism and responsible party are explicit.
- **Free quote.** Universal and low-information.

### What appears more persuasive to this buyer

- **Comparable WA project evidence.** Place, loads, power, storage, solar, generator and what changed. Statewide and PowerPlus use this well.
- **A visible path from uncertainty to scope.** OffGrid WA's package ranges, calculator and information-rich form tell the buyer how the process starts.
- **Single-point accountability.** “The same party designs, installs and supports it” is a stronger promise than a battery feature when failure affects water, refrigeration, communications, livestock or business operations.
- **Respect for the generator.** Treating it as designed redundancy rather than a moral failure sounds more operationally credible.
- **Specific local service proof.** Who answers, who travels, what can be diagnosed remotely and what happens when the system faults.

### White space RENOZ could test without overclaiming

1. **“Start with the property, not the battery.”** Show the scope inputs—location, current/future loads, peak starting currents, winter energy, existing solar, switchboard, generator and construction stage—then explain which parties do which work.
2. **“Know what is and is not in the solution.”** Make battery hardware price distinct from solar, inverter, generator, switchboard, structures, trenching, freight, travel, design, commissioning and support. Competitor package pricing creates demand for this explanation.
3. **“A WA project record, not a lifestyle testimonial.”** Publish system records with consent: problem, design constraint, architecture, equipment, installed responsibilities and operational observations. Avoid unverifiable savings extrapolations.
4. **“Generator policy by design.”** Explain auto-start, maintenance run, prolonged low-solar periods, fuel storage and which loads may remain generator-dependent.
5. **“Can this project be staged?”** Give honest boundaries for a live-in shed, new rural home, farm expansion or weak-grid property and identify which decisions must be made upfront to avoid stranded equipment.
6. **“Who owns the fault?”** Make the RENOZ/installer/inverter-manufacturer boundaries explicit before sale. This directly answers the channel-accountability gap in manufacturer-led offers.

### The most useful white space for one direct-response lead ad

Manufacturers mostly push hardware and installer referrals. Turnkey integrators mostly push packages and a quote. RENOZ can own the **decision-ready project brief** between them without pretending to be the installer.

**Ad proposition:** “Planning power for a regional WA property? Start with the property, not the battery.”

**Offer:** A short off-grid project-fit review that captures the property location, grid status or connection problem, current and planned loads, solar, generator, project stage, timing and budget readiness. RENOZ reviews the battery application and passes serviceable projects—with consent and the captured brief—to a suitable WA installer for complete design, quote and installation.

**Why this is competitively useful:**

- It answers the buyer's uncertainty before asking them to choose a package or product.
- It gives installers a materially better lead than a name-and-phone-number enquiry.
- It uses RENOZ's legitimate manufacturer role: battery application, compatibility, product support and installer enablement.
- It makes the complete solution cost boundary explicit instead of implying a low battery price equals a low installed price.
- It avoids a false promise of turnkey delivery by naming the installer as the party responsible for site assessment, system design, electrical works, quote, installation and commissioning.

**Minimum qualification fields:** postcode/property location; owner or authorised decision-maker; grid/no-grid/weak-grid status; connection quote if any; new build/existing property/expansion; essential and high-starting loads; existing solar and switchboard photos if available; generator status; single/three phase if known; target timing; realistic installed-project budget band or finance need; consent to share the brief with an installer.

**Proof beside the form:** one exact WA project record showing the constraint, property loads, solar/battery/inverter/generator architecture and named responsibility split. Avoid projected savings and lifestyle generalities.

## Red-team conclusion

The dangerous assumption is that a cheaper battery creates a cheaper customer decision. Competitors demonstrate the opposite: regional buyers are being sold engineering, installation, generator integration, travel, compliance, commissioning, remote support and risk transfer. Statewide and OffGrid WA can win even with a more expensive component because they make the *whole job* purchasable. PowerPlus and RedEarth can win because established installer ecosystems convert manufacturer trust into an installed outcome. genZ can win the ruggedness and institutional-safety heuristic. Renox can win the polished integrated-appliance and software heuristic.

RENOZ should not lead by asking the customer to believe its battery is cheaper. It should reveal whether it understands the property better, define the complete work and cost boundaries more honestly, and show who is responsible at each stage. Its defensible lead-generation role is to create a decision-ready project brief, qualify battery fit and route the opportunity—not to imply that the manufacturer is the licensed installer. If that handoff is not concrete, advertising will generate battery-price shoppers and technically complex leads that installers—not RENOZ—ultimately control.
